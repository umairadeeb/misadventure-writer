# Travel Craft

How to write the travel part. Humor sits on top of these; none of this works if the post is vague.

## 1. Never invent facts

This is the rule that protects the author's credibility.

- No made-up restaurants, hotels, prices, opening hours, events, distances, people, or quotes.
- If the notes don't say it happened, it didn't happen.
- Exaggeration for comedy is fine when it is obviously exaggeration: "a queue the length of a mortgage" is fine; "we waited 47 minutes" when nobody said so is not.
- Quotes: only use quoted speech that is in the notes, in the user's words. Paraphrase otherwise, or ask.
- Numbers: copy them exactly. If a figure looks off, flag it in the verify list.
- When something is missing, leave `[TODO: what time did the ferry actually leave?]` and ask.
- Do not "fill in" local history, culture, or food facts from memory unless the user supplied them or asked for them. If you add one, mark it `[VERIFY: ...]` in the output list.
- Inner states: the narrator's feelings come from the notes or the user. Do not manufacture "I felt a wave of peace" as fact.

## 2. Openings

Drop the reader into a moment already in motion. Never "Our trip to X began..." or "After months of planning...".

Good openings:
- Mid-scene, mid-mistake: "By the time I understood that the ferry was leaving without me, it was already leaving."
- A concrete object or line of dialogue from the notes
- A small confession

Check: could the first sentence open a different post about a different trip? If yes, it's too generic.

Avoid:
- Rhetorical questions ("Ever wondered what it's like to...?")
- Weather and scenery summaries as the first line
- Announcing the post ("In this post I'll tell you...")
- "Let me tell you about..."

## 3. Sensory detail

One specific sensory detail beats three adjectives. Smell, sound, texture, and temperature are underused.

- Brochure: "The market was a feast for the senses."
- Specific: "The market smelled of frying dough and, faintly, of someone else's cigarette."

Source of details: the notes and photo captions. If the user didn't mention a smell, don't invent one. Ask: "What did the place smell or sound like?"

## 4. Getting places, getting lost, logistics

Transport, queues, bad directions, and booking mix-ups are story material. They are where the narrator is most human and most funny. Don't skip them as filler.

- Give the exact failure: the wrong platform, the machine that took coins from a country the narrator had left.
- Cut logistics that are only logistics (what time the bus left) unless the time is the joke. Move useful logistics to the practical-info section.
- Keep the stakes honest. A missed train is funny when the cost was an afternoon, not when it was genuinely dangerous. If something was frightening or serious, see section 8 and the "when things really went wrong" note in `humor-playbook.md` section 9: soften the jokes and let the sincerity lead.

## 5. Structures

Pick based on the material, then the voice profile's preference.
- **One big story**: one mishap told start to finish. Best for a short, punchy post.
- **Themed**: groups moments by idea (food, transport, things that were closed). Good when no single story dominates.
- **Chronological day-by-day**: use short, titled sections (a funny and informative heading per day). Keep each day to one bit of material; don't narrate every hour.
- **List-shaped** ("5 ways I got lost in X"): fine if each entry is a real, specific story.

In every structure, find the turn (the moment the post exists for) and the sincere beat.

## 6. The practical-info section

An optional "the boring but useful bit" at the end (or wherever the voice profile says). Contents come only from the notes: where they stayed, rough costs, what they'd do differently, booking tips.

- Keep it plainly written: a short bulleted list is fine here.
- Tone: dry, brief, a single wry line allowed.
- No invented prices or opening hours. Mark unknowns `[TODO: price]`.
- Turn it on or off per the profile. Default: on for multi-day posts, off for short anecdotes.

## 7. Titles and excerpts

A good title is funny and still tells the reader where the post is.

- Include the place (or the type of trip) when possible: "How I Lost a Rental Car in Lisbon".
- Offer 3 alternatives that differ in approach (literal-funny, understated, punchline-first).
- Avoid: a pun in every title, clickbait ("You Won't Believe..."), all-caps, and title-case-with-colons.
- Excerpt: 1 to 2 sentences, in the narrator's voice, saying what happened without spoiling the best line.

## 8. Companions as characters

Companions can be characters only if the voice profile says so. Use the nicknames and the boundaries from the profile.

- Affectionate teasing about shared, real incidents is fine. Teasing about a person's body, health, finances, or relationships is not unless the profile explicitly allows it.
- If the notes don't say they'd mind, still keep the narrator as the main target.
- When something genuinely went wrong (illness, cancelled flight, a terrible hotel, a scare), put the human first: acknowledge it plainly, let humor come from the narrator's reaction and the absurd details, and never joke about someone's suffering. The post can be funny and kind at once: the joke is "I handled this badly", not "this was fun for everyone".

## 9. Endings

Don't wrap up with a moral or "it's an experience I won't forget". End on one of:
- A payoff of the running bit
- A small, concrete image
- A sincere sentence, plainly said
- A final understated joke that circles back to the opening

Skip the "until next time" sign-off unless the voice profile uses one.

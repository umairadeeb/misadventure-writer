# Anti-Slop: Two-Tier Rules

Goal: the post sounds like a specific person, not like a language model's average. Many generic anti-AI rules would also kill comedy, so this list has two tiers.

## Contents
1. How to use this file
2. Tier 1: hard bans
3. Tier 2: allowed when deliberate
4. Structural tells to check across a whole post
5. Never inject (when editing someone's draft)
6. The audit pass
7. Quick scoring

## 1. How to use this file

Run it twice: once in the de-slop pass and once in the final audit.
- **Tier 1** items are removed on sight. They are never funny and they always read as AI or as brochure copy.
- **Tier 2** items are devices. They are fine when they do comedic work (a punchline, a callback, timing, honest exaggeration) and are cut when they are filler. The audit must name the job of each one it keeps. "It sounds punchy" is not a job.

A post can pass Tier 1 and still be slop if every Tier 2 device is in service of nothing. Sparse is better than clean.

## 2. Tier 1: hard bans

### Dead vocabulary
delve, tapestry, nestled, bustling, vibrant, hidden gem, off the beaten path, a feast for the senses, a symphony of, kaleidoscope, melting pot, testament to, rich history, rich cultural heritage, treasure trove, picturesque, breathtaking, stunning, unforgettable, immerse yourself, embark on a journey, wanderlust, bucket-list-worthy, curated, seamless, elevate, unlock, foster, pivotal, crucial, landscape (abstract), navigate (abstract), realm, myriad, plethora, ever-evolving, game-changer.

### Brochure formulas
- "Whether you're a ... or a ..." and "Whether you're looking for X or Y"
- "From X to Y" used as a false range ("from ancient ruins to modern cafes")
- "Something for everyone", "a place where old meets new", "where tradition meets modernity"
- "A city of contrasts", "a land of contrasts"
- "Steeped in history", "boasts a", "offers a unique blend of"
- "Must-see", "can't-miss", "a must-visit"
- "Look no further"

### Significance inflation and vague authority
- "It's an experience you won't forget", "a trip I'll never forget", "memories that will last a lifetime"
- "Testament to", "stands as", "serves as a reminder", "plays a vital role", "underscores", "highlights the importance of"
- "Experts say", "many travelers", "locals say", "it is said that", "reviews suggest" with no source in the notes
- Fake grandeur about ordinary things ("a pivotal moment in my life") when the event was lunch

### Throat-clearing and announcements
- "Here's the thing", "Here's what nobody tells you", "Let me be clear", "Let me tell you", "The truth is", "Honestly?", "I'll be honest", "It turns out"
- "Let's dive in", "Buckle up", "Strap in", "Grab a coffee and settle in"
- "In this post, I'll...", "Without further ado", "As we'll see"
- "Spoiler:" and "Plot twist:" as automatic openers

### Chatbot and assistant residue
- "Great question", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like me to"
- Citation tokens, placeholder brackets left unfilled (other than intentional `[TODO]` and `[PHOTO]`), knowledge-limit disclaimers ("as of my last update")
- Meta-comments about the writing ("this section covers")

### Generic conclusions
- "In conclusion", "All in all", "At the end of the day", "In the end, it's not about the destination, it's about the journey"
- "Travel teaches us...", "This trip reminded me that..." (moral stapled onto a story)
- "Until next time", "Stay tuned", "Happy travels" as an automatic sign-off (unless the voice profile uses one)
- Any ending that is a summary of the post

### False agency and distance
- Things doing human verbs: "the city welcomes you", "the mountains call", "the sea whispers", "the day unfolds", "the decision emerges"
- A narrator floating above the scene: "Nobody designed this", "This happens because", "People tend to"
- Second-person stage direction: "Picture this", "Imagine standing..."

### Performed candor and manufactured stakes
- "Real talk", "No cap", "Not gonna lie", "Let's be honest", "I'll say it again"
- "In a world where", "now more than ever", "the stakes have never been higher"

### Formatting
- Bold as decoration on random phrases
- Emoji headings, emoji as punctuation
- Title Case On Every Heading
- Inline-header bullet lists ("**Food:** The food was...") in the narrative body
- Hashtag blocks in the body
- Curly/straight quote mixing

### Generic sensory filler
"The sights, sounds, and smells", "a sensory overload", "the aroma of spices wafted", "the sun-kissed", "golden hour glow" (without a real, specific observation)

## 3. Tier 2: allowed when deliberate

For each, the **job** that justifies it and the **filler form** that gets it cut.

| Device | Allowed job | Filler form (cut) |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence fragment | Punchline, beat of silence, comic understatement ("It was fine. It had my luggage.") | Dramatic runs of fragments for fake profundity ("A city. A story. A feeling.") |
| Group of three | Setup with a twist on the third item | Three similar adjectives, or a three used as a reflex every few lines |
| Hyperbole / "never", "every", "always" | Obvious exaggeration the reader recognizes as a bit | A sweeping claim about travel or people stated as fact |
| Em dash | Timing, an interrupted thought, a late reveal | A dash used as the default connector, several per paragraph |
| Rhetorical question | Voicing the reader's objection that the next line demolishes | A question opening the post or answered immediately in a "reveal" |
| "Not X, it's Y" contrast | Expectation versus reality when X is a real thing the narrator expected | A manufactured reframe ("It's not a hotel. It's a feeling.") |
| Parenthetical aside | The whispered thought the sentence couldn't say | Hedging, or a whole paragraph in brackets |
| Fourth-wall break | Preempting the reader's exact objection, once | A recurring "you know what I mean?" tic |
| Callback | Payoff of an earlier, real detail | Repeating a phrase to feel clever |
| Colloquial intensifiers ("absolutely", "frankly") | Voice, if the profile uses them and they carry the joke | Stacked "really", "truly", "genuinely", "incredibly" as emphasis |
| Short paragraph | Letting a line land | Every paragraph one sentence for drama |
| "Fake-casual" register ("So yeah.") | Only if the voice profile and samples use it | An all-purpose chat tone |

Judging "why is this here": look at the sentence without the device. If the meaning and the laugh survive, the device was filler. If the laugh dies, it was working.

## 4. Structural tells to check across a whole post

Check the post as a shape, not just sentence by sentence:
- **Same paragraph ending every time**: a one-liner zinger after every paragraph. Vary the landings.
- **Metronomic rhythm**: three or more consecutive sentences of the same length and structure.
- **Same opener runs**: several sentences or paragraphs starting identically.
- **Listicle-by-default** in a post that is meant to be a story.
- **Template arc**: setup, complication, moral, as if every post were produced by one template.
- **Uniform joke density**: a joke in every sentence is as much a tell as none. People are funnier in bursts.
- **A moral at the end** that the story never earned.
- **Synonym cycling**: "the establishment", "the venue", "the eatery" to avoid saying "the cafe" twice. Say "the cafe". Repetition of a plain word is fine.
- **Invented precision**: exact-sounding numbers or times the notes didn't give.
- **Fake specificity**: details that sound concrete but could belong to any trip ("a charming little cafe").
- **Over-balanced**: every complaint immediately softened with "but it was worth it".
- **Missing mess**: nothing is ever uncertain, unflattering, or unresolved. Real stories have loose ends.

## 5. Never inject (when editing someone else's draft)

The cure for slop must not add a stock "human" kit. Do not add, when the source didn't have it:
- A fabricated personal experience ("I've seen this a hundred times")
- A fake contrarian foil
- Performed candor
- Staccato fragments to manufacture rhythm
- Invented specifics (names, numbers, dates, quotes)
- A joke that isn't about the narrator, to hit a quota

## 6. The audit pass

Ask in order:
1. **Tier 1 sweep**: search for every banned word and formula. Remove each.
2. **Tier 2 justification**: list each fragment, three, hyperbole, dash, rhetorical question, and aside. For each, name its job. Cut those with none.
3. **Shape check**: run the section 4 list.
4. **What still sounds AI?** Read the first and last sentence of every paragraph. Which ones could appear in any trip post?
5. **What would the author cringe at?** If a profile exists, check against its banned words and "never says" list.
6. **Weakest joke**: pick it. Cut it unless you can sharpen it.
7. **Second sweep**: the tells that survive rewrites are contrast formulas, tidy one-line closers, forced triads, and dashes. Search for them again.

## 7. Quick scoring

Score 1 to 5. Anything below 4 gets another pass.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Voice match | Does it sound like the profile's author, not a generic funny writer? |
| Laughs per paragraph | Roughly one real laugh, uneven distribution, a few sincere paragraphs? |
| Slop | Tier 1 clear? Every Tier 2 device justified? |
| Grounding | Every fact traceable to the notes? |
| Kindness | Nobody punched down at? Narrator is the main target? |

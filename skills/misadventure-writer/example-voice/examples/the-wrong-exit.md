# The Wrong Exit

*A fictional sample post for the demo voice. Not a real trip.*

I was holding a baguette in a laundry room when I understood I had taken the wrong exit.

To be fair to me, the sign said "Sortie," and I am a person who believes a sign. It did not say "Sortie, but only for people who live here and own a washing machine." A woman looked up from a basket of towels and said something kind and quick. I replied in the single French sentence I had been rehearsing for three days, which was about the weather. It was not about the weather.

Bea found me four minutes later. She had gone out the correct exit, walked around the building, and located me through what I assume was sisterly instinct and what she says was "the baguette, Dot, you could see it from the street."

My plan for the day had been simple. Collect the key to the apartment. Find a bakery. Take a photo of myself near a bakery. The first step involved a lockbox, a code, and a paper map I had decided to read without my glasses. A decision was made.

I'd like it noted that the map was a very good map. It had labels. It had a compass rose. I simply read the compass rose as a suggestion.

The apartment turned out to be a fourth-floor room with a ceiling that sloped like a tent, one window, and a view of a neighbor's sock, which hung from a line for the entire week and never dried. We named it Gerald. Gerald endured rain, wind, and a pigeon. Gerald got more of a vacation than I did.

On the second night I walked back from dinner alone because Bea had gone ahead, and I got lost for the fourth time that day. I stopped under a streetlamp, turned the map the other way up, and a man walking a very small dog stopped beside me and, without a word, pointed down the street I'd been standing next to. Then he nodded, as one nods to a colleague, and kept walking.

I stood there longer than I needed to. Somewhere there is a man who, every evening, rescues strangers from their own maps and does not expect anything for it. I would like to be that man. I would also like to own a very small dog.

I found the building. Bea had left the hall light on.

**The boring but useful bit**
- The apartment's lockbox code was emailed the day before. Screenshot it. I did not.
- Bring the glasses.

The bread roll I bought that morning is still in my bag. We have an understanding.

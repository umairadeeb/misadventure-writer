# The Hotel With Opinions

*A fictional sample post for the demo voice. Not a real trip.*

The brochure called it "a tranquil mountain retreat." The retreat was a three-story building with the soul of a bus station and a man at reception who asked, as we checked in, whether we were "the two with the cheap booking." We were. He wrote this on a card and put the card in a drawer. It was the most organized thing I saw all week.

Our room was on the third floor, which in this building meant the fourth floor, because the second floor was called the first floor and the first floor was a mystery. The shower had two settings: Arctic and a personal grudge. The bed had been built by someone who had read about beds and not met one. At 2 a.m. something above us began to drag furniture, slowly, in a pattern I can only call theatrical.

I was prepared to be annoyed. I am good at annoyed. But by the second night I'd noticed that every complaint I made out loud to Bea was answered by a man I never saw, who left a small fix in the hallway. A second towel. A thermos of tea. On the third morning, a note, handwritten, that said, in confident English: "Shower is angry. We hit it with wrench."

It was not fixed. But it was a gesture, and it made me laugh for the first time in three days.

On the last evening the dragging noise stopped. I went up to the roof to see if the building had anything to show me and found a very old woman and a very old cat, sitting on two chairs, looking at the same mountain. She nodded at me. I nodded back. The mountain did what mountains do when people stop talking, which is quietly out-class everyone.

I don't know what the dragging was. Bea thinks it was a table. I think it was a lifestyle.

When we left, the man at reception handed me a card from the drawer. It said "the two with the cheap booking," and under that, in different handwriting: "good guests." I put it in my passport. It is the nicest review I've ever received, and the shower still doesn't work.

**The boring but useful bit**
- Shoulder season rates were genuinely low. So was everything else.
- Bring earplugs. Not for the dragging. For the thinking about the dragging.

# Four Hours on a Ferry I Did Not Need

*A fictional sample post for the demo voice. Not a real trip.*

The ticket said "Pier 3." The ferry I boarded was at Pier 4. These are neighboring numbers, which is how I knew I was close to the right answer and also in the wrong country, spiritually.

I realized this about nine minutes into a four-hour crossing, when the boat left and the island I wanted did too.

I'll say in my defense that I had a snack. It was a large snack, planned for the actual trip, which I now ate in a fit of grief by the second hour. Bea, who had boarded the correct ferry forty minutes earlier and was texting me photos of a harbor, wrote: "How's the ocean?" It was fine. It was big. It was the most ocean I'd had access to, and I used none of it.

The ferry had a small cafe with a menu in two languages and a barista with the calm of someone who has watched many people discover they're on the wrong boat. I ordered a coffee and, because my confidence had left with the island, pointed at things on the menu instead of speaking. I received a coffee, a cheese pastry, and an expression of deep patience.

Around the third hour I did the only reasonable thing: I stopped fighting it. I went up to the open deck, found a bench, and watched the water go from gray to green to a color I'd only seen on a paint card. A woman beside me was knitting something enormous. Neither of us spoke. At one point a bird landed on the railing and looked at me with open contempt, and I felt, for the first time that day, understood.

We got in late. Bea met me at the dock with a coat I had not asked for and had needed since the second hour. "Four hours," she said. "On purpose?" I said it was an act of commitment. She said it was an act of reading comprehension. We were both right.

It cost me one day of the trip and a sandwich's worth of dignity. I'd do it again, though not on purpose, and not at Pier 4.

**The boring but useful bit**
- Check the pier number. Then check it again, out loud, to a stranger.
- The cheese pastry was good.

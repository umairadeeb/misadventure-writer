# Voice Profile: "Dot Fennimore" (FICTIONAL DEMO)

> This is a made-up voice that ships with the skill so it works out of the box. Dot, her sister, her blog, and every trip in `examples/` are invented. Run voice setup to replace this with your own.
> Overall confidence: High (it is a character, so it is consistent by design).

## Basics
- **Audience:** friends, family, and a few strangers who found her by accident
- **Platform:** plain Markdown
- **Spelling:** American
- **Typical length:** 500 to 900 words
- **Preferred structure:** one big story, with a short practical section at the end on longer posts
- **Swearing:** mild ("hell", "damn", the occasional "crap")

## Taste
- **Loves:** dry British travel humor, deadpan memoir, anyone who can describe a bad hotel in one sentence
- **Finds cringe:** "wanderlust" captions, relentlessly cheerful travel influencers, puns in every heading

## The narrator
- **Self-deprecating angles:** terrible with directions, overpacks "just in case" items that are never the right case, confidently wrong about foreign-language signs, cheap to the point of self-sabotage, convinced she can read a paper map
- **Joke styles:** dry understatement for big disasters; full operatic alarm for small ones; deadpan lists; the comic three with a turn
- **Default temperament:** mostly understates, then overreacts to one tiny thing per post

## Rhythm and mechanics
- **Sentence rhythm:** medium sentences, then a short one to land the joke. Rarely more than two short sentences in a row.
- **Punctuation habits:** parentheses for the whispered thought (two or three per post). Em dashes rare. Almost no exclamation marks. One at most, for a joke.
- **How openings work:** drops straight into a moment mid-mistake. Example: "I was holding a baguette in a laundry room when I understood I had taken the wrong exit."
- **How endings work:** a small concrete image or a callback to the running bit, never a moral. Example: "The bread roll is still in my bag. We have an understanding."
- **How jokes land:** the funny word is last. Callbacks are planted early in a sentence that is not itself a joke.
- **Where the sincere moment goes:** about two-thirds in, plain, no joke at the end of the paragraph
- **Sign-off:** none

## Vocabulary
- **Uses:** "reasonably", "technically", "in my defense", "a decision was made", "I would like it noted"
- **Never uses:** "wanderlust", "hidden gem", "bucket list", "journey" (as a noun for a trip), "amazing", "epic", "literally", "game-changer"
- **Pet phrases and recurring bits:** keeps an "emergency bread roll" in her bag on every trip; refers to her phone's map app as "the oracle"; calls bad decisions "a decision was made"

## People
- **Travel companions:** her sister, Bea, who plans well and is patient about Dot not doing so
- **Allowed as characters?** yes, as the competent foil who is never mocked for anything but a shared, real incident
- **Off-limits about them:** Bea's job, her weight, her relationship status

## Boundaries
- **Off-limits topics overall:** no jokes about illness or injury while it is happening; no jokes on anyone's accent, language ability, or local customs
- **Built-in rule (always on):** never punch down at locals, cultures, accents, religion, or poverty.

## Practical info section
- **Include?** only on posts longer than 700 words
- **Placement:** end, under the heading "The boring but useful bit"
- **Tone:** plain, short bullets, one dry line allowed
- **Contents:** where she stayed, rough costs she has actually paid, one thing she'd do differently

## Quoted examples (all fictional)
- **Opening:** "I was holding a baguette in a laundry room when I understood I had taken the wrong exit."
- **A joke landing:** "The ticket machine offered four languages and I failed in all of them."
- **A self-deprecating line:** "I packed sunscreen, a phrasebook, and unearned confidence."
- **A sincere moment:** "The lake went quiet at dusk and neither of us said anything, which for Bea and me is a sort of record."
- **Closing:** "The bread roll is still in my bag. We have an understanding."
